The Ballad of Salt and Stone is a National Heritage Lottery funded project working with heritage organisations in Sandwich and Deal. Alison Ramsey and Kate Valentine from Digital Drama along with Carolyn Oulton ran workshops for local residents to uncover, preserve and share memories of living in these vibrant East Kent communities. This project enabled participants to develop reminiscence, story-telling and interpretation skills to foster confidence and enhance employability, whilst preserving heritage. Local volunteers co-facilitated reminiscence sessions, gained skills in oral history recording and creative writing, to capture, interpret and preserve memories of older residents. Together they created a community ballad and a short film, featuring the newly recorded oral histories and archival material from museum collections.

Project Activities and Outputs:



● Recruit 8 volunteers from Deal and Sandwich (4 in each town) to represent different ages and lived experiences from community groups

● Train 8 volunteers in oral history, reminiscence and creative writing skills

● Run 2 reminiscence sessions for 40 people (one in each of the towns) using handling collection and archive photographic material from the partner museums

● Record 12 oral histories from residents of Deal and Sandwich to capture memories of coastal town life in living memory for local project partners' museum archives

● Run 2 writing workshops to introduce the archive material and recordings to inspire a creative response to the local memories and heritage of the two towns

● Use the material created in the writing workshops as the basis to co-create a ballad, Salt and Stone, that reflects the distinct heritage of the 2 towns, mentored by creative writing expert Professor Carolyn Oulton

● Record the ballad with local performers, singers and musicians and upload it to Canterbury Christ Church University’s storytelling digital space to listen to via an interactive map for each location

● Digitise archive material from museum partners to be used in a short film and held in the collections for public access in the future

● Create a short film featuring extracts of the new oral histories, archival footage, and photographs of the coastal town

● Upload the film to Canterbury Christ Church University’s storytelling digital space for a global audience

● Run 2 sharing events (one at each of the partner museums) to watch the film and gather further memories of living in the coastal communities of north-east Kent.
